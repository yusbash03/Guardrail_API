# User Personas

## P1 — Amara, the busy professional (primary demo persona)

- 34, product manager, shops online weekly for groceries and household items.
- **Goal:** stop spending Sunday evenings building the same grocery basket.
- **Fear:** an AI that "helpfully" buys premium brands and blows the budget.
- **Needs:** firm weekly grocery cap, approval for anything over $30, only trusted stores.
- **Success looks like:** "I type one sentence, check one approval card, tap PayPal, done — and I can see exactly what's left."

## P2 — Daniel, the household budget owner

- 41, shares a budget with a partner; tracks every category.
- **Goal:** category-level control (groceries vs. household vs. personal care) with monthly limits.
- **Fear:** duplicate orders and purchases at odd hours.
- **Needs:** duplicate-purchase protection, time-of-day restrictions, a transparent ledger and audit trail.
- **Success looks like:** monthly report that reconciles to the cent with PayPal.

## P3 — Ngozi, the cautious first-time AI user

- 27, curious about AI agents but doesn't trust them with money.
- **Goal:** try AI shopping with zero risk.
- **Needs:** "approve everything" mode (threshold $0), blocked new merchants, plain-language explanations.
- **Success looks like:** the agent explains *why* a purchase was blocked, in her words, citing her own rules.

## P4 — Hackathon judge (secondary, but critical)

- Has ~3–5 minutes per project.
- **Needs:** to see real PayPal Sandbox checkout, a real AI tool loop, and proof the AI can't bypass rules.
- **Success looks like:** an obvious "the AI tried, the policy said no" moment, a verified webhook landing, and a ledger that updates live.

## Anti-persona

- Someone wanting the AI to buy automatically with no human involvement on each payment. The MVP intentionally requires buyer approval in PayPal for every order.
